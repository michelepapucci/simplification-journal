How the g1 models use their control token

How g1 · guided (with g1 as a check) represents its control token layer by layer, whether that predicts what the output reaches, what “no level” stands for, whether lower levels make De Mauro’s basic vocabulary more probable, and which kinds of words and punctuation the token makes more or less probable. What we found

  • The levels form one graded scale, in every layer. From layer 3 on, levels 0–80 lie in order along the main direction (ρ = 1), every level sits between its neighbours, clustering always merges adjacent levels first, and distance grows with the gap between levels (ρ 0.93–0.99). The requested level can be read back from the state with R² ≥ 0.99 in layers 7–17. It is a single scale, not separate groups.
  • In the middle layers the level is what the state mostly encodes. In UMAP and t-SNE maps of single originals, in layers 10–16, 73–88% of a point’s neighbours share its level (±10), and only 10–19% its original. Before layer 8 and after layer 18 the sentence’s content dominates (about 80% of neighbours are the same original, the level is at chance). With each original’s own mean removed, the levels order cleanly at every layer.
  • The scale is widest at the bottom, but the outputs don’t follow. The step from level 0 to 10 is about 1.9 times the average step in the model, yet an ordinary step in the outputs, and request 0 still lands at 21–23. The state also predicts the outcome no better than the request (R² 0.24–0.27 against 0.26–0.28): the shortfall and the spread happen while writing.
  • “No level” behaves as level 40. Its state is nearest to level 40’s at every layer, and its outputs’ READ-IT matches request 40’s best (mean 42.6 against 41.9). Textually its output is no closer to the level-40 output than to the others: two samples differ more than two neighbouring levels do.
  • Basic vocabulary barely varies in the data, and not at all in the model. In the g1 training simplifications, the share of De Mauro basic words goes only from 90.3% (level 70) to 92.5% (level 0). The models’ texts are 91–92% basic at every level (originals 90%), and their next-token probability on basic words stays at 88.5–89%: they keep the average rise, not the gradient.
  • The token changes structure, steadily from 40 to 20 to 0. Read under level 0 instead of 40, the same text’s commas become half as probable (×0.49), subordinating conjunctions and adverbs ×0.74, pronouns ×0.82, verbs ×0.87 and adpositions ×0.89, while nouns, proper nouns, numerals, determiners and auxiliaries stay put; ending the text becomes 3.2 times more probable at every step, and full stops 1.4 times. The outputs show the same shifts as the real simplifications: from request 70 to 0, auxiliaries 4.0% → 7.4% (references 3.5% → 7.6%), pronouns 3.8% → 1.8% (4.2% → 1.5%), adverbs 3.8% → 2.1% (4.0% → 2.0%).

How it is computed

  • Models: g1 · guided and g1 (Part 2 of the explorer). Hidden states: the residual stream after the embeddings and after each of the 28 blocks, in float16.
  • Ordering: Spearman ρ between level and the first principal component of the 9 trained level means (0–80). In-between: share of triples a < b < c with a and c further apart than either is from b. Neighbour merges: share of the merges of average-linkage clustering that join adjacent levels.
  • Readout: ridge regression with 5-fold cross-validation, folds by original. Projections: MDS (scikit-learn, metric), t-SNE (perplexity 30, after PCA to 50 dimensions) and UMAP (15 neighbours), every second layer.
  • Word classes (E): the fixed texts tagged with stanza 1.11.0 (tokenize, mwt, pos); each word’s probability is the product of its vocabulary pieces’ probabilities, the pieces assigned to words by character offsets; a contraction takes its first part’s tag. End of text, full stop, comma, semicolon and colon are single vocabulary items, so their probabilities at every step are exact.
  • Vocabulary: word forms lemmatised with stanza 1.11.0 (the profiler’s pipeline) on the g1 texts and the test outputs; a form is basic if its lemma or the form is in NVdB 2016, as in READ-IT; proper nouns, numbers and foreign words are left out. A vocabulary item counts as a word when it is a space followed by letters; words split into pieces and apostrophe forms are not counted.
  • Code: experiments/interp/ on master (states.py, lexicon.py, logits.py, classes.py, projections.py, analyze.py); this page by scripts/build_interp_webview.py.
