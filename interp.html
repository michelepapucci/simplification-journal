How the g1 models use their control token

Four questions on g1 · guided, with g1 as a check: how the control token is represented, layer by layer; whether the representation predicts what the output reaches; what “no level” stands for; and whether lower levels make De Mauro’s basic vocabulary more probable. Back to the explorer.

Findings A. The levels, layer by layer B. Readout C. “No level” D. Basic vocabulary

What we found

  • The levels form one graded scale, in every layer. From layer 3 on, levels 0–80 lie in order along the main direction (ρ = 1), every level sits between its neighbours, clustering always merges adjacent levels first, and distance grows with the gap between levels (ρ 0.93–0.99). The requested level can be read back from the state with R² ≥ 0.99 in layers 7–17. The token alone, and its input embedding, are ordered too. It is a single scale, not separate groups.
  • The scale is widest at the bottom, but the outputs don’t follow. The step from level 0 to 10 is about 1.9 times the average step in the model, yet in the outputs it is an ordinary step (0.7–1.0 times the average), and request 0 still lands at 21–23, two levels too high. The model tells the strongest requests apart; the shortfall happens while writing.
  • The state predicts the outcome no better than the request (R² 0.24–0.27 against 0.26–0.28): beyond the request, where an output lands is sampling noise.
  • “No level” means level 40. Its state sits where level 40 would be, like the never-trained level 90, and its outputs land at 42.6 on average, the same as request 40. Guidance pushes away from this middle point, which is why it stretches the curve in both directions.
  • Lower levels do not make basic vocabulary more probable. 88.5–89% of the probability on whole words goes to De Mauro’s basic vocabulary at every requested level, the token alone moves it by less than 0.2 points, and guidance lowers it slightly. The outputs’ words are 91–92% basic at every level, while the real simplifications go from 91% (level 70) to 94% (level 0).
  • The token changes structure instead. Against “no level”, level 0 makes ending the text 3.1 times more probable, a full stop 1.4 times, and sentences opening with an article (Il, La, Le) 1.3–1.5 times; it makes semicolons (×0.28) and openings with a preposition or adverb (Per, Durante, Con, In: ×0.3–0.6) less probable. Level 70 does the reverse.

How it is computed

  • Models: g1 · guided and g1 (Part 2 of the explorer). Hidden states: the residual stream after the embeddings and after each of the 28 blocks, in float16.
  • Ordering: Spearman ρ between level and the first principal component of the 9 trained level means (0–80). In-between: share of triples a < b < c with a and c further apart than either is from b. Neighbour merges: share of the merges of average-linkage clustering that join adjacent levels.
  • Readout: ridge regression with 5-fold cross-validation, folds by original. Level 90 is never a training target and g1 never sees “no level”: their rows keep their initial value and are shown as references.
  • Vocabulary: word forms lemmatised with stanza 1.11.0 (the profiler’s pipeline) on the g1 texts and the test outputs; a form is basic if its lemma or the form is in NVdB 2016, as in READ-IT; proper nouns, numbers and foreign words are left out. A vocabulary item counts as a word when it is a space followed by letters; words split into pieces and apostrophe forms are not counted.
