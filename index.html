Simplification explorer

A Qwen3-0.6B model asked to rewrite an Italian sentence at a requested READ-IT global level, with one control token, as in the LREC 2026 setup. The training pairs come from the Wikipedia part of IMPaCTS. Part 1 analyses the data, and Part 2 the models.

Dataset

Earlier work with three control tokens (READ-IT base, lexical and syntax; datasets v0 to v7): archived explorer.

How dataset g1 was built

The training pair is an IMPaCTS original and one of its simplifications. Its control token is the READ-IT global level of the simplification: level = 10 · ⌊10 · score⌋, so a simplification at 43.7 is a <|readability_40|> example. The full construction, with every count, is in data/g1/README.md on the master branch.

81,058 pairs from 10,762 originals; 1% of the originals (807 pairs) are the validation split. Levels 10–60 have 9,900–12,000 pairs each, levels 0 and 70 about half that; a one-level drop is as common as a five-level one (12,863 pairs). The test set is 1,000 held-out originals (418 at level 70, 582 at 80), each requested at every level below its own: 7,582 requests. READ-IT global is noisy: two near-identical simplifications of the same original differ by 3.8 points at the median and 9.2 at the 75th percentile.

Part 2

The models

Three Qwen3-0.6B models trained on g1 are being trained and evaluated: g1 (plain), g1 · balanced (levels 0–70 shown equally often) and g1 · guided (with a “no level” token, for classifier-free guidance at generation time). Their results will appear here.
